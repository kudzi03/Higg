# Higgsfield Reference Pack — Quality Bar for VelaBuilt ONE SECOND

These five clips are **quality-bar references**, not shot-for-shot templates.

## What to study

Use them to reassess ONE SECOND before spending more credits. Pay attention to:

- camera language and motivated movement
- performance and character presence
- shot diversity and lens choice
- camera proximity and wide-angle energy
- macro product/detail photography
- environmental storytelling
- physical interaction and tactile motion
- editorial rhythm and transitions
- sound design, dialogue/voice, and how audio drives cuts
- how the piece avoids feeling like an AI still-image slideshow
- how ordinary/small-business subject matter is made to feel like a complete brand world

## What NOT to do

- Do not copy the food-truck/burrito concept, characters, exact shots, framing, dialogue, or sequence.
- Do not use these clips as permission to imitate a specific campaign.
- Do not simply add more volumetric light, particles, contrast, or generic "cinematic" adjectives to the current H1 frame.

## The comparison problem

The current bakery H1 is technically coherent, but the concern is that it reads as a **beautiful AI-generated still** rather than a frame from an exceptionally directed commercial.

Checkpoint 2 proved the depth/parallax pipeline works technically. Preserve that work, but treat frozen-depth motion as **one filmmaking technique**, not the dominant language of the film.

Before Checkpoint 3, reassess where actual generative video buys visible production value: performance, food physics, complex camera movement, environmental motion, macro interaction, and moments that cannot be faked convincingly with 2.5D treatment.

## Core question

Do not ask only: "Can this be made technically convincing cheaply?"

Ask:

**"Would someone watching this genuinely struggle to believe a tiny creative operation made it?"**

The production target remains disciplined: approximately 25 total project credits where possible. Credit efficiency should come from intelligent shot selection, reuse, compositing, and local processing—not from lowering the filmmaking standard.
