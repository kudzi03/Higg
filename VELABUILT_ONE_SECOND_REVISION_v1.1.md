## 1. Spoken lines

### What's wrong with v1
| Line | Verdict |
|---|---|
| VO1 "Every morning, I get one second." | Explains the concept out loud, which the timestamps already do. Voice-of-brand, not a person. **Cut.** |
| VO2–4 "One second to be noticed. / To be trusted. / To be remembered." | A classic triad manifesto, interchangeable with a hundred brand films. **Cut.** |
| D1 "Wait — this is you?" | Natural, but it points at vanity (*you*) rather than the place. **Revise.** |
| D2 "Every morning." | The best line in the film: specific, proud, and it means "that ad is real, it's what I do every day." **Keep.** |
| Super "Your customers never see your budget." | Useful as a proposition, but manifesto-flavoured. **Move to the direct-response cut, reworded.** |

### Three alternatives

**A. Craft murmurs (recommended)**
Each owner, frozen mid-task, says a few private words about their own work, the kind of perfectionism customers never see. It isn't narration and it isn't addressed to the viewer. It's what they're thinking in that frozen second.

| Time | Who | Line | Delivery |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:01.2 | Baker | *"Too much glaze."* | Under her breath, self-critical, a little amused. |
| 0:05.5 | Skincare founder | *"Three drops. Never four."* | A private ritual, near-whisper. |
| 0:10.5 | Welder | *"Steady… steady."* | Low, talking to the metal. |
| 0:26.0 | Customer | *"Is that here?"* | Looking from the phone to the counter. Real curiosity. |
| 0:27.6 | Baker | *"Every morning."* | Not looking up. Quiet pride. |

*14 spoken words.*

Why it wins:
- The lines show care instead of claiming it. Business owners recognise themselves ("that's me with the glaze"), and that's what makes them want to inquire.
- **The baker's voice opens and closes the film**, so the viewer knows the soft-focus woman in the reveal is the woman from the ad by ear, not by her face. That makes the reveal land harder and covers any small identity drift between the AI hero frame and the reveal plate.
- *"Is that here?" / "Every morning."* turns the ending into a claim of truth: the beautiful version is real, made from what she already does. That answers the biggest objection to polished ads (that they're fake) without saying it.

**B. The silent second (least narration)**
No VO at all. Frozen worlds carry only sound design and timestamps. The only words in the film are *"Is that here?" / "Every morning."* (5 words).
- *Strengths:* the most confident and most cinematic option, works best with the sound off, and needs only two voices.
- *Weakness:* the skincare and steel worlds become beautiful images without a person behind them, which pushes back toward the portfolio-montage feel you want to avoid. The baker's voice is heard only once, so the reveal loses its voice link.

**C. One observation**
A single line from the baker at the top, then silence until the reveal: *"Most people walk past in about a second."* Then *"Is that here?" / "Every morning."*
- *Strengths:* the premise is clear at once, and the line is concrete and true for any shop.
- *Weakness:* it explains the concept in the first two seconds, which kills the mystery, and it still sounds like an opening thesis.

**Recommendation: A.** CP0, the free animatic, will A/B **A against B** by muting the murmurs, so the final choice is made by watching both, at zero cost.

**Sound-off handling (both A and B):** murmurs appear as small lowercase monospace captions next to the speaker (*too much glaze.*), settling letter by letter like everything else that's frozen. Dialogue in the reveal gets standard burned-in captions.

## 2. Brand line

**What it has to do:** describe the result rather than the process, be specific without being a slogan cliché, link to the frozen moment, and be short enough to sit under a wordmark.

| Rank | Line | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| **★ 1** | **Worth stopping for.** | Three meanings at once: time stopped, the scroll stops, a passer-by stops and walks in. It promises the result, not the tool. Borrowed warmth from the "a place worth stopping for" idiom. Rarely used in ad-tech marketing. |
| 2 | **Look like the business you already are.** | The honesty angle: VelaBuilt doesn't fake you, it shows the quality that's already there. Pairs perfectly with "Every morning." Slightly long for a tagline, so it's **used as the direct-response headline instead.** |
| 3 | **Your business, at full size.** | A sharper version of "look bigger than you are" (you already *are* this; you just look smaller). Distinctive, slightly cryptic. |
| 4 | **You do the work. We make it seen.** | Clear and humble, but service-copy flat. |
| 5 | **Make your one second count.** | The tightest link to the concept, but "make it count" is a worn idiom. |

**Rejected:** anything with *elevate, unleash, reimagine, next-level, powered by, stories that stick, magic*; "Big-brand quality, small-business price" (a price claim, and it cheapens the brand); "The care, made visible" (pretentious).

**Descriptor (under the wordmark):** *Flagship campaigns for independent businesses.* "Independent" is specific and warm, and it says who the client is. "Flagship" says the quality level. It replaces "every size", which says nothing.

*Before lock:* a quick conflict check on "Worth stopping for" (trademark and usage search, free).

## 3. The phone reveal as a hero shot

### Why these reveals usually look fake
A screen that's too clean and too bright · no reflections · a pasted perspective · a fake "wiggle" shake · a background that's too beautiful · hands generated by AI · the foreground and background moving as one flat card · audio that doesn't change · and above all, **the zoom-out-into-a-phone transition, which viewers now recognise as a template.**

### Structural fix: cut on the bell, don't zoom out
- In V1, the baker turns toward the shop door. At 0:24.8, **the door bell rings in the ad.**
- At 0:25.0: **hard cut** to the over-the-shoulder reveal. A *different* bell (a cheaper bell in real room acoustics) rings as someone enters the real shop. On the customer's phone, **the same shot keeps playing, frame-continuous** (the screen shows V1's next frame).
- The score collapses to a tinny phone speaker on the same frame.
- The viewer understands instantly from the sound and the matching image. A sound-bridge cut is more confident, and much harder to spot as a trick, than a scale animation.

### Production path: make the reveal actually real
**Path A, practical (recommended, 0 credits).** The reveal is meant to look ordinary and real, so we film it for real.
- **Location:** any small bakery or café, with permission, for about 30 minutes.
- **Shot:** phone A films phone B. Phone B plays the V1 export at full brightness.
- **Phone A settings:** 4K, 24 or 25 fps, 1/50 shutter. Exposure locked for the room, focus locked on phone B's screen. Use the 2× or 3× lens so the background goes soft.
- **The customer:** over-the-shoulder, holding the phone slightly below chest height, tilted about 6° (not centred).
- **Background:** at 3–4 m, a person in a dark apron with hair tied back, side or back to camera, boxing pastries.
- **Also capture while there:** 30 s of room tone, the real door bell, and a clean background plate without the phone.
- **What it gives us for free:** real reflections, real moiré, real hands, real focus falloff, real handheld motion and real sound.
- **Gold version:** photograph the location and the person first, then generate H1 using those photos as references. The ad and the reveal then show *the same real bakery and the same real person*. That's the product proven literally, and it can become a real case study. **Needs a signed release from the location and the person.**

**Path B, fully generated (fallback, about 8 credits: H6 plus a V2 plate).** If A isn't possible, every one of these is required:
1. **Screen physics:** screen brightness slightly overexposed relative to the room, with highlight bloom; display gamma and saturation shift; a pixel grid visible only on the closest frames; rounded corners and a generic bezel.
2. **Reflections:** a blurred, flipped crop of the room plate itself on the glass, sliding opposite to the hand's motion, plus a faint fingerprint-smudge layer catching the window light.
3. **Screen-light spill:** the screen lights the customer's thumb and fingers, flickering with the clip's per-frame brightness. **This one detail does more for realism than any other.**
4. **Real handheld motion:** the camera motion is tracked from a 10-second phone video you shoot anywhere (free; OpenCV feature tracking), not a synthetic wiggle. Applied with depth-correct parallax: foreground phone at 1.0×, background at 0.35×.
5. **A focus pull:** a partial rack from the phone to the counter at 0:28.5 as the customer glances up. The baker stays soft. A focus pull is the strongest "real camera" signal there is.
6. **The hand:** the phone hides most fingers, only a thumb at the screen edge, so there's no full AI hand. The foreground phone and hand are a still layer, so they can't warp.
7. **Ordinary imperfection:** mixed fluorescent and daylight, slight green cast, a little underexposed; a crumpled receipt, a paper bag and a tip jar; one blurred person crossing the far background; asymmetric framing.
8. **One optical layer over the whole composite:** grain applied once after compositing (never per layer), the same vignette, slight barrel distortion and edge chromatic aberration.
9. **A generic video UI:** a progress bar and neutral icon shapes, with no platform branding or recognisable layout.
10. **Audio placement:** the phone audio is mono, band-limited (350 Hz–4.5 kHz), placed at the phone's position with early room reflections. Real or CC0 café ambience under it.
11. **Composition rule:** the phone screen fills about 22–28% of the frame. Big enough to read, small enough not to be a "screen insert shot."
12. **The 3-viewer test:** before lock, show a still and the 6-second clip to three people who know nothing about the project. If anyone says "AI", "edited" or "fake", fix it before anything else.

## 5. Are all four businesses necessary? No. Cut the hotel.

| Business | What only it provides | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Bakery** | The protagonist, the unfreeze and the reveal | **Essential** |
| **Skincare** | The most aspirational register ("Dior"), the drop-into-drop transition (the film's signature move), and a core VelaBuilt target market (small DTC brands that actually buy ads) | **Keep** |
| **Steel** | The surprise. A trades workshop made spectacular proves "ordinary businesses" more strongly than anything else; it's the most memorable frame. | **Keep** |
| **Hotel** | City scale and the coffee → glaze match | **Cut** |

**Why cutting the hotel makes the film better:**
- **Pacing:** each frozen world gets 5–6 s instead of about 4 s. Frozen images need time to feel luxurious rather than rushed.
- **The unfreeze comes earlier:** at **0:20 instead of 0:22**, which helps retention. The reveal gains 1 s.
- **Rule of three:** the structure is easier to remember.
- **A better transition:** the hotel route needed two transitions (sparks → city lights → coffee → glaze). The new single move, **sparks drift, cool and slow, and become the gold dust floating in the bakery's light shaft**, is a stronger material match: bright particles hanging in light, in both worlds.
- **No overlap:** hospitality overlapped with the bakery (food, warmth, morning coffee).
- **Savings:** one hero still, one optional 4.5-credit video, one voice and one transition build.

*What we lose:* the "one city" scale and the oldest owner. The timestamps still say "same second." The hotel returns in the 16:9 or longer version, or in a hospitality-targeted cut.

## 7. When to stop (no reroll spiral)

**Global rules:**
1. **No third attempt on any asset** without your explicit OK.
2. **No unchanged rerolls.** Every retry must change one variable we've diagnosed (prompt, end frame, model or duration), written down before it's submitted.
3. **Two failures in a row on the same asset means stop**, and we diagnose together.

### Gate 1: does the look work? (CP1–CP2, at most about 5 credits spent)
- **Look development:** up to 8 Soul Cinema frames for H1 (about 1 credit). If none passes the rubric below, I get 2 Nano Banana Pro attempts (4 credits). **If still none passes: stop.** The look isn't reachable at this budget, and about 5 credits are lost.
- **2.5D test (0 credits):** if an uninvolved viewer calls it a "photo slideshow" or "Ken Burns", we **stop and decide together** whether to switch the frozen worlds to video. That raises cost, so it needs re-approval and never continues automatically.
- **H1 rubric (all must pass):**
  - It could sit next to a top-tier food-campaign frame without apology.
  - The light is single-source and motivated.
  - At 100% zoom, hands and face have no AI artefacts.
  - The flour and glaze look physically believable.
  - The composition holds at 9:16 inside the safe areas.
  - The amber-and-blue palette is there.

### Gate 2: the kill point (CP3, the V1 hero unfreeze)
- **Exact point:** after the **second** V1 attempt. Cumulative spend at that moment is **24 credits at most.**
- **Pass:** at least **2.5 s of continuous usable footage from frame 0**. That means:
  - no face morphing and no hand mutation
  - the baker's motion is natural
  - the flour and glaze behave plausibly
  - no visible jump from the H1 still
- **If neither take passes, flagship production stops.** I present options and do not run them:
  - (a) a comp-only "time resumes" (a particle-simulated flour cloud, with the baker held still while the camera moves; weaker)
  - (b) one test on Cinema Studio v2 with a speed-up ramp (5 credits), only with your approval
  - (c) shelve the concept at about 24 credits spent, keeping about 61
- **Why here:** V1 is the payoff *and* the image on the phone in the reveal. If the unfreeze doesn't land, everything else is decoration. It's also the first significant spend.

### Gate 3: the reveal (CP5)
- **Path A footage** must pass the 3-viewer test after one comp revision.
- **If it fails:** one Path B plate (6 credits). If that fails too, we ship the flagship ending without the over-the-shoulder shot and end on the unfreeze plus the end card, so the film is still finished.

### Budget tripwire
**If spend reaches 20 credits before V1 is approved, stop and review**, whatever the gate status.
